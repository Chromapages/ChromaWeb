**Project:** Chromapages Marketing / Business Website  
**Project type:** Premium marketing, positioning, proof, qualification, and lead-generation website  
**Planned stack:** Next.js, TypeScript, Sanity CMS  
**Status:** Pre-build discovery brief

**Proof doctrine:** Evidence before adjectives.

---

# 1. Purpose of This Brief

This discovery brief establishes the strategic foundation for the Chromapages website before information architecture, copywriting, wireframing, visual design, CMS implementation, or development begins.

Its purpose is to align the project around:

- who Chromapages is for
- what problem the website must help sell against
- what buyers value
- what makes Chromapages different
- what the website must accomplish commercially
- what claims can and cannot be made
- what content and proof are currently available
- what remains unknown
- how the site should guide qualified visitors
- what should be measured after launch
- what decisions must be resolved before implementation

The website should not become a digital brochure describing everything Chromapages can do.

It should function as a **commercial decision system** that helps the right buyer:

**Recognize the problem → understand the consequence → see a credible solution → trust the process → evaluate proof → choose an offer → take the next step**

# 3. Core Business Problem

The website must sell against a specific condition:

> **The business has evolved faster than its digital presence.**

The company may have:

- improved its services
- increased pricing
- entered new markets
- added locations
- expanded its offer
- become operationally more sophisticated
- developed a stronger reputation
- begun attracting more valuable customers

But the digital experience may still communicate:

- an older business
- a smaller business
- a less differentiated business
- a less sophisticated offer
- a weaker customer experience

This is the **Digital Mismatch**.

The website project should therefore not frame the buyer's problem simply as:

> “You need a better website.”

The deeper problem is:

> **The digital experience no longer represents or supports the business accurately.**

---

# 4. Core Buyer Pain

## Primary Pain

> **The business has become better, more credible, or more ambitious than the experience customers encounter online.**

This creates a perception gap between:

**what the company actually is**

and

**what the website makes it appear to be.**

---

## Commercial Consequence

The mismatch may make it harder for a prospective customer to:

- understand the offer
- understand what makes the company different
- trust the company
- find relevant proof
- navigate the available services
- identify the correct next step
- complete that next step efficiently

The website must discuss these consequences carefully.

Do not automatically convert them into unsupported claims of:

- lost revenue
- lost leads
- conversion decreases
- ranking losses
- ROAS changes

without verified evidence.

---

## Buying Anxiety

The second major pain is not about the website itself.

It is about the redesign project.

The buyer may think:

> **We know the website needs to change, but we don't want to spend serious money on another redesign that only looks better or becomes difficult to finish.**

This creates two simultaneous buying requirements:

### Better result

The buyer wants:

- stronger perception
- better clarity
- better mobile experience
- stronger customer paths
- greater flexibility
- better performance

### Safer engagement

The buyer wants:

- clear scope
- defined responsibilities
- controlled approvals
- reliable communication
- QA
- launch planning
- predictable handoff
- continued support where appropriate

Chromapages must sell both.

# 11. Buying Triggers

A weak website alone does not create urgency.

Discovery and marketing should look for a **trigger event**.

Priority triggers include:

- rebrand
- repositioning
- price increase
- move upmarket
- new service
- new market
- new campaign
- paid traffic
- new location
- multi-location expansion
- catering growth
- franchise ambitions
- vendor failure
- fragile website infrastructure
- outdated CMS
- significant leadership frustration with the current site

A prospect with both:

**Digital Mismatch + Trigger**

should generally receive more priority than one with a poor site but no reason to act.
