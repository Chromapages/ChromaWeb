# Content Strategy

## Purpose
This document defines the content strategy for the Chromapages marketing website, including page-by-page content goals, copy approach, messaging priorities, and CTA strategy.

This is not a design document. It is a content and conversion document. Its job is to make sure every page has a clear role in moving the visitor toward trust, qualification, and action.

---

## 1. Content Strategy Goals

The website content should help Chromapages:

- explain what the business does in clear language
- attract qualified leads
- build trust quickly
- communicate premium positioning without sounding vague or inflated
- show that Chromapages understands business outcomes, not just websites
- reduce poor-fit inquiries
- move visitors toward discovery calls or qualified contact submissions

---

## 2. Core Messaging Principles

### 1. Lead With Outcome
Visitors care less about features than results. Content should focus on:
- more leads
- better conversion
- stronger credibility
- easier growth
- cleaner launches
- ongoing support

### 2. Stay Clear And Human
The tone should be:
- clear
- confident
- human

Avoid jargon-heavy agency language or generic buzzwords.

### 3. Make The Business Easy To Understand
The visitor should quickly understand:
- what Chromapages does
- who it helps
- why it is different
- what happens next

### 4. Sell With Structure, Not Hype
The content should build trust through:
- positioning
- proof
- process
- offer clarity
- expectation setting

### 5. Every Page Needs A Job
No page should exist just because “agency sites usually have one.” Every page should serve one or more of these functions:
- explain
- qualify
- prove
- convert

---

## 3. Core Brand Message

### Supporting Differentiators

### What The Content Must Reinforce
Chromapages is not just selling deliverables. It is selling:

## 4. CTA Strategy

## Primary CTA
**Book A Call**

This should be the dominant CTA across the site.

### Why
The site is meant to generate qualified conversations, not passive browsing. A call is the clearest conversion step for warm traffic and qualified inbound leads.

## Secondary CTA
**View Work**

This supports users who need proof before they are ready to inquire.

## Supporting CTAs
Depending on page context, these may also be used:
- Contact Us
- Get A Quote
- See Our Work
- Explore Services
- Start Your Project

### CTA Rules
- one primary CTA focus per page
- keep CTA language direct
- match CTA to user intent
- do not overload pages with too many competing actions
- every major page should have at least one meaningful next step

---

## 5. Copy Tone

### Tone Attributes
- Clear
- Confident
- Helpful
- Direct
- Professional
- Non-corporate

### What The Tone Should Avoid
- sounding overly technical
- sounding too casual or sloppy
- generic agency filler
- exaggerated claims
- empty positioning language

### Preferred Writing Style
- short paragraphs
- strong headlines
- plain English
- outcome-first language
- specific claims over vague adjectives

### Examples Of Good Tone
- “We build websites that help you get more customers.”
- “Your site should not just look better. It should convert better.”
- “Clear process. Clean launch. Ongoing support.”

### Examples To Avoid
- “We optimize omnichannel digital ecosystems.”
- “Transformative solutions for scalable synergy.”
- “Revolutionary web experiences that redefine innovation.”

- explain process

View Work

---

## Services

### Page Goal
Help users understand what Chromapages offers and where they fit.

### What This Page Must Do
- explain each service area clearly
- reduce confusion around what is offered
- guide users toward the right engagement path
- support internal linking to detail pages

### Content Priorities
1. Service categories
2. What each service solves
3. Who each service is for
4. Outcomes and scope framing
5. CTA to contact or go deeper

Book A Call

Show that Chromapages works in a structured, low-chaos way.

3. Who each option is for
4. What influences investment
5. FAQ

### Secondary CTA

- clarify who the business works best with

## Privacy Policy

### Page Goal
Support legal clarity and trust.

### Content Notes
This page should remain straightforward and easy to navigate, but it does not need marketing-heavy copy.

---

## FAQ Strategy

### FAQ Role Across The Site
FAQs should help answer objections before they block conversion.

### Common FAQ Topics
- timeline
- investment
- what’s included
- whether existing sites can be improved
- whether Chromapages helps with copy
- support after launch
- how the process works
- whether the site will support SEO and paid traffic

### FAQ Writing Rules
- keep answers short
- answer the actual concern
- avoid over-explaining
- end with a logical next step where helpful

---

## 7. Internal Linking Strategy

Content should support natural movement between:
- homepage and services
- services and detail pages
- work and case studies
- pricing and contact
- about and contact
- process and contact

### Key Principle
Every important page should help the user do one of three things:
- understand the offer better
- trust the business more
- take the next step

---

## 8. Content Prioritization For Launch

### Must-Have
- homepage copy
- core services copy
- process copy
- pricing framing
- about copy
- contact copy
- basic FAQs
- project summaries
- key CTAs

### Nice-To-Have

---

## 9. Content Maintenance Strategy

### Short-Term
- keep core pages accurate
- improve work proof as projects mature
- refine pricing language based on lead quality
- add stronger FAQs based on sales conversations

### Medium-Term
- add service detail pages
- add case studies
- add landing pages for campaigns
- add industry pages if vertical focus sharpens

### Long-Term
- expand blog/resource content
- build topic clusters around priority services and industries
- use content to support paid and organic growth together

---

## 10. Final Recommendation

The content strategy should stay focused on one thing: making Chromapages easier to trust, easier to understand, and easier to hire.

If a piece of copy does not improve clarity, proof, qualification, or conversion, it probably does not need to be there.
